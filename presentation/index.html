02 · The signal

Don't look for leaks.
Look for anomalies.

Leaks are rare and unlabelled. Ordinary ground is abundant. So we model ordinary ground, then subtract.

01

Expect

Predict the surface from weather, soil, land cover, season and vegetation.

ŷ = f(rain, T, ET₀, soil, NDVI, …)
02

Subtract

Observed minus expected gives a two-sided residual — wetter or drier than it should be.

z = (y − ŷ) / σ
03

Persist

Keep only patches that stay wet through the dry season, on or near a mapped pipe.

score = 0.55·wet + 0.25·persistence + 0.20·cool
03 · Method

One pipeline.
Four places.

Every study area runs the same code. Only the configuration changes: AOI, grid, orbit, network and labels.

01

Ingest

Sentinel-1 RTC, Sentinel-2, Landsat, ERA5, soil, land cover, DEM and the pipe network.

5 sensors · one grid
02

Expectation

LightGBM learns what each 20 m pixel should look like under those conditions.

VV · NDMI regressors
03

Residual

Observed minus expected. Robust z-scores, dry-season mean and persistence.

two-sided anomaly
04

Fusion

Moisture, persistence and thermal cooling, weighted by distance to the pipe.

60 m plume scale
05

Triage

Ranked inspection candidates with evidence cards, maps and honest validation.

not a detector

06 · Results

The model works.
The detector doesn't.

Skilful surface prediction does not automatically become a leak detector. These are held-out test scores, not training fit.

LABEL RANKING
0.52–0.55

area under the ROC curve across the three labelled cities

RANDOM 0.50

Why the gap?

  • A repair point marks where a pipe failed — not where a persistent moisture plume survived.
  • A 20 m pixel is 400 m²; a 0.1–0.6 m pipe is a sliver of it.
  • Irrigation, crops, roads and snow produce the same surface signature.

What would change our mind?
  • A confirmed leak with before/after acquisitions
  • Calibrated NISAR L-band HH/HV and closure phase
  • Hydraulic data and metre-scale field validation
  • More labels that separate repair events from persistent wet patches

HARD LIMITS WE DO NOT PAPER OVER

20 m pixels cannot localise a metre-scale leak. Thermal data cannot resolve a pipe-scale halo. A weak label ranking is not a detection. The right output is a ranked inspection list — and a clear statement of uncertainty.
